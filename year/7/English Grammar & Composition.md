# English Grammar & Composition Master Textbook — Age 7 (Primary Level / Class 2)

## 1. Learning Philosophy & Objectives

### Philosophy & Vision
At age 7 (Class 2 / Advanced Primary Level), language becomes a vehicle for critical analysis, intellectual depth, literary storytelling, persuasive reasoning, and eloquent spoken oratory. This textbook provides a rigorous 40-week curriculum covering all 8 parts of speech in depth, advanced tenses (Simple, Continuous, and introduction to Perfect), prefixes/suffixes, idioms, formal & informal letters, 3-paragraph essay composition (100–150 words), and 3-to-5 minute public debate/speech presentation.

### Core Learning Milestones
1. **Advanced Grammar & Syntax Mastery:**
   * Collective Nouns (*a pride of lions, a constellation of stars*), Abstract Nouns (*honesty, courage, wisdom*), Irregular Plural transformations.
   * Demonstrative, Reflexive & Relative Pronouns (*who, which, whose, myself, himself*).
   * Tense Triad: Simple Present, Simple Past (Regular `-ed` & 40+ Irregular verbs), Simple Future (*will/shall*), and Present/Past Continuous.
   * Adverbs of Manner, Place, Time, and Frequency (*quickly, nearby, yesterday, always*).
   * Prepositions of Direction & Movement (*across, through, toward, into, onto*).
   * Coordinating (FANBOYS) & Subordinating Conjunctions (*because, although, unless, while*).
   * Quotation marks, direct speech formatting, commas in apposition, and apostrophes.
2. **Literature Appreciation & Comprehension:** Reading chapter books (Guided Reading J–N / Lexile 350L–550L), identifying Theme, Tone, Character Motives, Cause-and-Effect, and Fact vs. Opinion.
3. **Structured Creative & Expository Composition:** Writing 3-paragraph Narrative, Descriptive, Persuasive, and Letter formats.
4. **Vocabulary & Word Mechanics:** 50 Latin/Greek prefixes & suffixes (`un-, dis-, re-, pre-, -ful, -less, -ment, -ness`), 20 classic English idioms.
5. **Public Oratory & Debate:** Delivering 3–5 minute persuasive speeches and formal debates on real-world topics.

## 2. 40-Week Comprehensive English Reader & Composition Curriculum

### Term 1: In-Depth Grammar & Tense Formations (Weeks 1–10)

---

#### Week 1 — Nouns In-Depth: Concrete, Collective & Abstract Nouns
* **1. Concrete Nouns (Perceived by 5 senses):** marble, rose, perfume, thunder, water.
* **2. Collective Nouns (Names for a group of things/people/animals):**
  * *A flock of birds* 🐦 | *A pride of lions* 🦁 | *A school of fish* 🐟 | *A swarm of bees* 🐝
  * *A herd of cattle* 🐄 | *A fleet of ships* 🚢 | *A bouquet of flowers* 💐 | *A constellation of stars* ⭐
* **3. Abstract Nouns (Ideas, qualities, feelings — cannot touch physically):**
  * **honesty** (truthfulness), **bravery** (courage), **kindness**, **wisdom**, **patience** (Sabr), **justice** (Adl), **freedom**.
* **Sentence Modeling:** "The young boy showed great **bravery** and **honesty** in the face of danger."

---

#### Week 2 — Advanced Plurals & Irregular Noun Morphologies
* **Vowel Mutation Plurals:**
  * man -> **men** | woman -> **women** | foot -> **feet** | tooth -> **teeth** | goose -> **geese** | mouse -> **mice**
* **Nouns ending in -f / -fe (change to -ves):**
  * leaf -> **leaves** 🍃 | wolf -> **wolves** 🐺 | knife -> **knives** 🔪 | life -> **lives** | shelf -> **shelves** | thief -> **thieves**
* **Foreign & Zero Plurals:**
  * sheep -> **sheep** 🐑 | deer -> **deer** 🦌 | fish -> **fish** 🐟 | aircraft -> **aircraft** ✈️ | child -> **children** 🧒 | ox -> **oxen** 🐂

---

#### Week 3 — Possessive Nouns: Singular vs. Plural Apostrophes
* **Singular Possessive (Add 's):**
  * The bag of the boy -> *The boy**'s** bag.*
  * The crown of the king -> *The king**'s** crown.*
* **Regular Plural Possessive (Add only ' after s):**
  * The school of the boys -> *The boys**'** school.*
  * The nest of the birds -> *The birds**'** nest.*
* **Irregular Plural Possessive (Add 's):**
  * The toys of the children -> *The children**'s** toys.*
  * The club of the women -> *The women**'s** club.*

---

#### Week 4 — Pronouns Review: Demonstrative & Relative Pronouns
* **Demonstrative Pronouns:**
  * **This** (singular, near 👈) | **That** (singular, far 👉)
  * **These** (plural, near 👈👈) | **Those** (plural, far 👉👉)
* **Relative Pronouns (Joining sentences):**
  * **Who** (for people): *This is the truthful boy **who** returned the lost wallet.*
  * **Which / That** (for animals/things): *I read the book **which** explained the solar system.*
  * **Where** (for places): *Makkah is the holy city **where** the Kabah stands.*

---

#### Week 5 — Main Verbs vs. Auxiliary (Helping) Verbs
* **Primary Auxiliaries:**
  * **Be verbs:** is, am, are, was, were, being, been.
  * **Do verbs:** do, does, did.
  * **Have verbs:** has, have, had.
* **Modal Auxiliaries (Ability, permission, necessity):**
  * **can / could** (ability: *I can speak English fluently.*)
  * **may** (permission/politeness: *May I assist you, father?*)
  * **must** (obligation: *We must speak the truth always.*)

---

#### Week 6 — Simple Present Tense & Universal Truths
* **Uses:** Daily habits, routines, and universal scientific facts.
* **Universal Truths:**
  * *The Earth revolves around the Sun.*
  * *Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.*
  * *Honesty brings inner peace and divine blessings.*
* **Subject-Verb Agreement Drills:**
  * *The sun (shine) brightly.* -> *The sun **shines** brightly.*
  * *They (pray) together in congregation.* -> *They **pray** together in congregation.*

---

#### Week 7 — Simple Past Tense: Regular & Irregular Verb Catalog
* **Regular Verbs (Add -ed / -d):**
  * walk -> walked | visit -> visited | plant -> planted | pray -> prayed | cook -> cooked.
* **40 Essential Irregular Verbs (Memorization Table):**
  * go -> **went** | see -> **saw** | eat -> **ate** | drink -> **drank** | write -> **wrote** | read -> **read** (/red/)
  * buy -> **bought** | bring -> **brought** | teach -> **taught** | think -> **thought** | catch -> **caught**
  * give -> **gave** | take -> **took** | make -> **made** | find -> **found** | fly -> **flew** | speak -> **spoke**
  * tell -> **told** | hear -> **heard** | know -> **knew** | stand -> **stood** | sit -> **sat** | win -> **won**
* **Story Transformation:** Rewrite a present tense paragraph into past tense!

---

#### Week 8 — Simple Future Tense: Will / Shall + Base Verb
* **Formula:** `Subject + will/shall + Verb (base form)`
* **Examples:**
  * *Tomorrow, we will visit the National Science Museum.*
  * *I will strive hard to become a compassionate doctor.*
  * *InshaAllah, our team will win the quiz competition.*

---

#### Week 9 — Continuous Tenses: Present Continuous & Past Continuous
* **Present Continuous (Now):** `is/am/are + Verb-ing`
  * *The students **are conducting** a fascinating chemistry experiment.*
* **Past Continuous (Ongoing in the past):** `was/were + Verb-ing`
  * *While the birds **were singing**, the shepherd **was watching** his flock.*

---

### Term 2: Adverbs, Prepositions, Conjunctions & Mechanics (Weeks 11–20)

---

#### Week 11 — Degrees of Comparison of Adjectives (Rules & Exceptions)
* **Rule 1 (Add -er and -est):**
  * bright -> brighter -> brightest | fast -> faster -> fastest
* **Rule 2 (More & Most for 2+ syllable words):**
  * beautiful -> **more beautiful** -> **most beautiful**
  * generous -> **more generous** -> **most generous**
  * courageous -> **more courageous** -> **most courageous**
* **Irregular Comparisons:**
  * good -> **better** -> **best** 🌟
  * bad -> **worse** -> **worst**
  * many/much -> **more** -> **most**
  * little -> **less** -> **least**

---

#### Week 12 — Adverbs: Types & Functions (How, Where, When, How Often)
* **1. Adverbs of Manner (How? — Usually ends in -ly):**
  * slowly, quickly, gracefully, quietly, courageously, neatly, politely.
  * *The brave soldier fought **courageously**.*
* **2. Adverbs of Place (Where?):**
  * everywhere, nowhere, inside, outside, upstairs, nearby.
  * *The birds searched for food **everywhere**.*
* **3. Adverbs of Time (When?):**
  * yesterday, today, tomorrow, now, soon, early.
* **4. Adverbs of Frequency (How often?):**
  * always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never.
  * *A true believer **always** tells the truth.*

---

#### Week 13 — Prepositions of Movement & Direction
* **Prepositions:** across, through, into, onto, toward, along, over, past.
* **Examples:**
  * The brave diver jumped **into** the cool water.
  * The train zoomed **through** the dark mountain tunnel.
  * We walked **along** the sandy beach.
  * The eagle soared **over** the high snowy peaks.

---

#### Week 14 — Conjunctions: Coordinating (FANBOYS) & Subordinating
* **Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS):**
  * **F**or (reason) | **A**nd (addition) | **N**or (negative choice) | **B**ut (contrast) | **O**r (alternative) | **Y**et (surprise contrast) | **S**o (result).
* **Subordinating Conjunctions (Complex Sentences):**
  * **Because** (*He succeeded because he worked with dedication.*)
  * **Although** (*Although it was raining, the farmers harvested the crops.*)
  * **Unless** (*You cannot enter unless you have permission.*)
  * **While** (*She read quietly while her brother painted.*)

---

#### Week 15 — Punctuation Masterclass & Direct Speech Dialogue Formatting
* **Direct Speech Rules:**
  1. The exact words spoken are enclosed in **Quotation Marks (" ")**.
  2. A **comma (,)** separates the speaker tag from the spoken quote.
  3. The first letter inside the quotation mark must be **Capitalized**.
  4. The terminal punctuation (. / ? / !) goes **inside** the closing quotation mark.
* **Example:**
  * Bilal said, **"Knowledge is the greatest treasure in the world."**
  * **"Where are you traveling tomorrow?"** asked mother.

---

#### Week 16 — Prefixes and Suffixes (Word Morphology)
* **Prefixes (Added to the beginning):**
  * **un-** (not): unhappy, untruthful, unkind, safe -> unsafe.
  * **dis-** (opposite/not): disagree, disappear, dishonest.
  * **re-** (again): rewrite, rebuild, recycle, recharge.
  * **pre-** (before): preheat, preview, prehistoric.
* **Suffixes (Added to the end):**
  * **-ful** (full of): careful, peaceful, joyful, thankful.
  * **-less** (without): careless, fearless, hopeless, harmless.
  * **-ly** (in a certain manner): quickly, softly, honestly.
  * **-ment / -ness** (state of being): excitement, kindness, darkness.

---

#### Week 17 — Advanced Synonyms & Antonyms (Academic Vocabulary)
* **Synonym Pairs:**
  * ancient = historic | gigantic = colossal | furious = enraged | cautious = careful | brilliant = intelligent | wealthy = affluent.
* **Antonym Pairs:**
  * courageous ❌ cowardly | humble ❌ arrogant | permanent ❌ temporary | abundant ❌ scarce | triumph ❌ defeat.

---

#### Week 18 — Classic English Idioms & Figurative Language
* 1. **Piece of cake:** Very easy. (*"Solving this math riddle was a piece of cake!"*)
* 2. **Break the ice:** Start a friendly conversation in a new group.
* 3. **Once in a blue moon:** Happening very rarely.
* 4. **A blessing in disguise:** Something that seemed bad at first, but turned out good.
* 5. **Actions speak louder than words:** What you do is more important than what you say.

---

#### Week 19 — Similes & Metaphors (Poetic Language)
* **Similes (Using 'as...as' or 'like'):**
  * *As brave as a lion* 🦁 | *As white as snow* ❄️ | *As busy as a bee* 🐝 | *Runs like the wind* 💨
* **Metaphors (Direct comparison):**
  * *The camel is the ship of the desert.* | *Books are keys to the universe.*

---

* 100-mark assessment on Adverbs, Prepositions, Conjunctions, Direct Speech, Prefixes/Suffixes, and Idioms.

### Term 3: Literary Reading Comprehension & Rhetoric (Weeks 21–30)

---

#### Week 21 — Reading Comprehension 1 (Historical Narrative): "The Noble Sultan Salahuddin"
* **Full Text:**
  > *In the twelfth century, Sultan Salahuddin Ayyubi ruled with legendary justice and mercy.*
  > *During the intense battles of the Crusades, his fierce rival, King Richard of England, fell dangerously ill with a burning fever.*
  > *Instead of taking military advantage of his weakened enemy, Sultan Salahuddin dispatched his personal royal physicians to treat the King.*
  > *He also sent cooling mountain snow and fresh sweet fruits across enemy lines to soothe Richard's fever.*
  > *King Richard was utterly astonished by this unparalleled act of chivalry and compassion.*
  > *When Salahuddin eventually reclaimed the holy city of Jerusalem, he forbade any harm to innocent citizens, protected all places of worship, and granted safe passage to everyone.*
  > *His sublime character proved that true victory lies not in hatred, but in justice, honor, and magnanimity.*
* **Comprehension Questions:**
  1. How did Sultan Salahuddin respond when he learned King Richard was ill?
  2. What items did he send across enemy lines to relieve the fever?
  3. How did Salahuddin treat the citizens when he entered Jerusalem?
  4. What profound lesson does this historical event teach about Islamic chivalry (Futuwwah)?

---

#### Week 22 — Reading Comprehension 2 (Science & Nature): "The Secret Dance of the Honeybees"
* **Full Text:**
  > *Deep inside the intricate hexagonal combs of a beehive, an extraordinary communication system exists.*
  > *When a scout honeybee discovers a rich field of blooming lavender miles away, she returns to the hive to share the location.*
  > *Instead of making sounds, the scout bee performs a specialized choreography known as the 'Waggle Dance.'*
  > *By vibrating her abdomen and moving in a figure-eight pattern, she communicates the exact angle relative to the sun and the precise distance to the flowers!*
  > *Within minutes, hundreds of worker bees decipher the code and fly directly to the nectar-rich flowers.*
  > *This marvelous precision is a testament to the divine wisdom instilled by the Creator in the smallest of creatures.*
* **Comprehension Questions:**
  1. What is the name of the dance bees use to communicate? (The Waggle Dance).
  2. What two vital pieces of information does the dance convey? (Direction relative to the sun and exact distance).
  3. What geometric shape do honeybees build their honeycombs in? (Hexagonal).

---

#### Week 23 — Reading Comprehension 3 (Biography): "Ibn al-Haytham — The Father of Modern Optics"
* **Text & Legacy:**
  > *Over a thousand years ago in Basra and Cairo, the brilliant polymath Al-Hasan Ibn al-Haytham revolutionized human knowledge.*
  > *Before his discoveries, ancient Greeks believed that the eye emitted rays of light to see objects.*
  > *Ibn al-Haytham conducted rigorous dark-room experiments (Kitab al-Manazir) and proved that vision occurs when light rays reflect off objects and enter our eyes.*
  > *He invented the 'Camera Obscura' (Pinhole Camera) and established the modern Scientific Method based on empirical experimentation and peer verification.*

#### Week 25 — Analytical Summarization: The 5-Finger Retell Framework
* **Thumb:** Characters (Who is involved?)
* **Index:** Setting (Where and when did it happen?)
* **Middle:** Problem (What obstacle arose?)
* **Ring:** Events (What three steps happened to solve it?)
* **Pinky:** Resolution & Theme (How did it end and what did we learn?)

---

#### Week 26 — Cause and Effect Relationships
* **Cause (Why it happened):** *The heavy monsoon rains overflowed the riverbanks...*
* **Effect (What happened as a result):** *...therefore, the green agricultural fields were flooded with fresh alluvial silt.*
* **Signal Words:** because, as a result, consequently, therefore, since.

---

#### Week 27 — Fact vs. Opinion Discrimination
* **Fact (Proven by evidence):** *The sun is a burning sphere of gas.* / *Water freezes at 0°C.*
* **Opinion (Personal belief/feeling):** *Winter is the most enjoyable season.* / *Blue is the prettiest color.*
* **Exercise:** Classify 10 statements as Fact or Opinion.

### Term 4: 3-Paragraph Essays, Formal Letters & Debates (Weeks 31–40)

---

#### Week 31 — Descriptive Essay: "My Sanctuary in Nature" (3-Paragraph Structure)
* **Paragraph 1 (Introduction):** Introduce the setting (a serene garden by a crystal-clear stream) and capture reader interest with a strong hook.
* **Paragraph 2 (Body - Sensory Symphony):** Describe sights (vibrant emerald moss, dancing butterflies), sounds (gurgling stream, whispering pines), smells (wet earth, blooming jasmine), and tactile breezes.
* **Paragraph 3 (Conclusion):** Reflect on the peace it brings to the heart and express gratitude to Allah for the sublime beauty of nature.

---

#### Week 32 — Narrative Essay: "The Day I Learned the Power of Patience"
* **Paragraph 1 (Beginning):** Setting the scene and introducing the challenge (e.g., trying to assemble a complex wooden model or learning a difficult Surah).
* **Paragraph 2 (Middle - Conflict & Climax):** The struggle, frustration, the temptation to quit, and mother's wise advice on *Sabr* (patience).
* **Paragraph 3 (Ending - Triumph & Moral):** Success through continuous effort and the lasting joy of perseverance.

---

#### Week 33 — Persuasive / Opinion Essay: "Why Tree Plantation is Vital for Our Planet"
* **Structure:**
  * **Thesis Statement:** *Planting trees is the most urgent and rewarding duty for every responsible citizen.*
  * **Reason 1:** Trees produce oxygen and absorb toxic carbon dioxide.
  * **Reason 2:** Tree roots prevent severe soil erosion and shelter wildlife.
  * **Call to Action:** *Let every one of us plant at least five trees this year to protect our green Earth!*

---

#### Week 34 — Friendly Letter to a Pen Pal Across the World
* **Format:**
  * 1. Sender's Address & Date
  * 2. Salutation (*Dear Yusuf,*)
  * 3. Introduction (*I hope this letter finds you in the best of health and iman.*)
  * 4. Body (Sharing details about my daily homeschooling routine, books, and science projects).
  * 5. Friendly Inquiries (*What is the weather like in your city? Which books are you reading?*)
  * 6. Sign-off (*Your loving friend, [Child's Name]*).

#### Week 36 — 3 to 5-Minute Public Speech: "Truthfulness — The Bedrock of Noble Character"
* **Speech Script & Delivery Guidelines:**
  > *"Respected teachers, honorable guests, and my beloved peers, As-Salamu Alaykum wa Rahmatullahi wa Barakatuh!*
  > *Today, I stand before you to speak on a timeless virtue that defines the dignity of human civilization: Truthfulness (As-Sidq).*
  > *Truthfulness is not merely speaking honest words; it is living an honest life. It means having an aligned heart, tongue, and deed.*
  > *Our beloved Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was known as 'As-Sadiq' (The Truthful) and 'Al-Amin' (The Trustworthy) even before he received revelation.*
  > *A single lie may seem like an easy escape, but like a web of shadows, it traps the soul in fear and guilt. Truth, on the other hand, grants unshakeable confidence, clean conscience, and divine light.*
  > *Let us make a solemn pledge today: no matter how difficult the situation, we will never utter a falsehood. We will stand tall with truth, for truth always prevails!*
  > *Thank you very much!"*
